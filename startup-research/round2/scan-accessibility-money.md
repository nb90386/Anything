# Where accessibility money is flowing now: what a solo founder can sell in weeks

Retrieved 2026-11-03. Method: 20 web searches. Most pricing comes from vendor pages, marketplace listings and government agenda documents. Anything marked **[unverified]** came from a secondary source only, or from my own reasoning.

## Correction to prior research (read this first)

- **HHS Section 504 was extended.** Several secondary sources report an HHS OCR interim final rule dated May 7, 2026, published in the Federal Register on May 11, 2026 as document 2026-09266. It moves the web and mobile compliance dates to **May 11, 2027** for recipients with 15 or more employees and **May 10, 2028** for smaller ones. One site (whatisada.com) still says the date was not extended. Check the Federal Register before using this in a pitch. **[secondary sources; the FR text was not fetched]**
- **The DOJ Title II extension** came as an April 20, 2026 interim final rule. Entities with a population of 50k or more now have until April 26, 2027, and smaller entities and special districts until April 26, 2028. DOJ says it intends further rulemaking during the extension that "could modify the substantive requirements". One source says the extension is being challenged in federal court. **This is a demand risk:** scope could narrow before the deadline arrives.
- Archived content and some preexisting documents may be exempt under Title II. Cities are actively sorting documents into fix, archive and take-down piles (ockem.com guide; San Rafael's "continuing content review" page). Backlogs will shrink through triage, so not every page gets remediated. This search found **no 2026 news of a city removing its meeting-video archive**. Only vendor warnings turned up.

## Who is making money, and what limits them

- **Incumbent bundlers win the city money:** CivicPlus/AudioEye DocAccess (Tuscaloosa, Clark County, Jurupa Valley, Whittier), Equidox (Alameda, Bellingham), Granicus (captions). Bellingham is piloting AI DocAccess and keeping Equidox for sensitive documents. Buyers are already sorting work into cheap AI remediation and higher-trust human remediation.
- **Marketplace sellers** are mostly India-based and price at $0.50–5/page. Top Fiverr and Upwork sellers quote custom prices for volume ("message me with your document count").
- **No solo operator publicly reports revenue** for PDF remediation or a remediation SaaS (Indie Hackers and Reddit searches found nothing). Treat any claimed solo-shop revenue as unknown.
- **Growth limit:** human minutes per page. Tables, forms, scanned pages and alt text drive cost; one specialist vendor quotes $30–80/page for complex budget documents. Automation is collapsing the simple tier (Stanford PREP auto-tags 90%+ of PDFs; Rev AI costs $0.25/min). Margin survives only where a human verifies the output and stands behind it, through a compliance report and screen-reader QA.

## Fastest realistic paths to $3–5k/month (60–90 days)

### Path 1: Fixed-price "Title II document sprint" for Bay Area cities with 50k+ population and their departments (best)
- **Offer:** remediate the top 300–1,000 public-facing PDFs (forms, agendas, fee schedules, applications) at $3–6/page. That undercuts the $4–11.50 list rates and stays below city-manager authority ($10k–50k). Include a tracking spreadsheet and PAC/veraPDF reports as the audit trail buyers want. The AI pipeline does tagging, reading order and alt-text drafts. The founder does manual QA.
- **Math:** two $2,500 jobs a month gets to $5k. That is about 600–800 pages a month, feasible only if AI does more than 80% of each page.
- **Why it's fast:** the April 26, 2027 deadline is about 6 months away. Sub-$50k purchases need no RFP in many California cities.
- **Blockers:** (a) **Age.** Contracts with a minor can be voidable, and cities may require a business license, W-9, vendor registration and certificates of insurance. A parent or adult co-signer, or an LLC with an adult member, is probably needed **[unverified; check with each city]**. (b) Incumbents already sell bundled DocAccess/Equidox subscriptions. (c) DOJ may narrow the rule's scope. (d) Quality liability: one bad deliverable kills references. (e) Cities triage documents to archive instead of fixing them.

### Path 2: Marketplace plus white-label supplier to web agencies
- **Offer:** a Fiverr/Upwork gig at about $3/page with 48-hour turnaround, plus cold outreach to agencies that build municipal, school and SMB sites, offering white-label remediation with a free 5-page sample (the model Skynet uses).
- **Math:** about 1,500 pages/month at $3/page is $4.5k. Agencies give repeat volume; marketplaces give the first reviews.
- **Blockers:** Upwork's terms require users to be 18+ and Fiverr has age rules too **[unverified, check the current ToS]**. Offshore price floors of $0.50–5/page. Marketplace ranking takes weeks of reviews. Agencies' overflow demand is unproven.

### Path 3: Caption QA for meeting-video backlogs (corrected AI captions)
- **Offer:** Whisper-class draft, human correction with civic vocabulary (names, motions, street names), and delivery of SRT/VTT files ready to upload to YouTube, Granicus or Swagit, at about $1–1.50/min. That undercuts Rev's $1.99 human rate and beats the accuracy of raw AI captions.
- **Math:** about 50 hours of video/month at $75/hr is $3.75k.
- **Blockers:** correction takes roughly 1–2x real time **[estimate]**, so this is labor-heavy. Granicus/Swagit lock-in, including bundled AI captions at about $60/hr (Elko). The archived-content exception may let cities skip old backlogs. Live captioning is a different product. Audio description is a poor add-on for council meetings.

**Recommendation:** run Path 1 as the core and use Path 2 for early reviews, case studies and cash flow. The same AI-plus-QA PDF pipeline serves both, and its per-page labor numbers later become the case for a self-serve SaaS. Before outreach, resolve the age and contracting question (adult co-owner or parent signatory).
