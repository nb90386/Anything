# Title II Digital Accessibility Demand Scan (US public entities)

Retrieved: 2026-11-03. Method: 16 web searches. Many entity items come from citizenportal.ai, which publishes AI-generated summaries of meeting transcripts. Treat those amounts as reported, not verified against the original agenda packets. Items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed from a second source.

## Verdict

**Urgency: high for entities of 50k+ population now. Medium for small entities, which have until 2028. Funding: real but small.**

- **Deadlines.** DOJ's interim final rule (Apr 20, 2026) moved compliance to **Apr 26, 2027** for public entities in jurisdictions of 50k+ and to **Apr 26, 2028** for smaller ones and all special districts. The WCAG 2.1 AA standard did not change. That leaves larger entities about 5.5 months.
- **Budgets.** Spending is happening across counties, cities and K-12 districts, but typical ticket sizes are **$7,500 to $60,000**. Most purchases are bundled SaaS tools (CivicPlus Doc Access plus AudioEye) bought through the incumbent website vendor. Few are bespoke remediation projects.
- **Funding is tight.** It is often unbudgeted or one-off. Story County's $7,500 contract was unbudgeted. Jurupa Valley used ARPA money. Nye County approved only $34,800 for immediate work and deferred its larger ADA plan. Umatilla County passed its contract on a 2-1 vote.
- **Higher education.** I found little contract-level evidence for public colleges in this scan (gap).

## 2. Removing content instead of remediating it

- **Clear Creek County, CO** "temporarily removed" older noncompliant documents as of June 30, 2025. It is remediating them to WCAG 2.1 AA and offers a request form in the meantime. ([link](https://www.co.clear-creek.co.us/1570/ADA-Website-Accessibility-Remediation))
- **North Dakota officials** have said content may be removed from government websites. ([Foster County Independent, Feb 23, 2026](https://www.fosterconews.com/story/2026/02/23/news/officials-say-content-may-be-removed-from-government-websites-to-satisfy-federal-accessibility-rule/7507.html))
  - Bismarck has taken down outdated content and keeps the files offline, available by records request.
  - Fargo has removed nothing and expects no removals, because older content is grandfathered.
- **Colorado's state IT office (OIT)** reportedly advises entities *not* to remove inaccessible documents. **[secondhand, via a vendor guide]**
- I found no public statement giving a document or page count for a specific entity's inventory. This is a gap; agenda packets are the place to look.

## 3. NFB lawsuit and DOJ posture

- **The case.** NFB v. DOJ/HHS was filed in D. Md. in May 2026 (sources say May 21 or May 27). It argues the extension skipped notice and comment and is arbitrary and capricious. It asks the court to restore the original dates. ([complaint](https://democracyforward.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NFB-vs-DOJ-Complaint.pdf), [NFB](https://nfb.org/images/nfb/publications/bm/bm26/bm2607/bm260703.htm), [Seyfarth](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/06/national-federation-of-the-blind-challenges-last-minute-deadline-extensions-for-website-and-mobile-app-accessibility/))
- **Summary judgment.** NFB moved for summary judgment Sep 28, 2026. Trackers report **no ruling as of Oct 12 to Oct 28, 2026**. Confirm on PACER before relying on this.
- **DOJ.** DOJ says it plans future rulemaking on the *substantive* requirements and may issue an NPRM. A tracker found no new rule as of Oct 14, 2026. I found no DOJ statement from Oct 2026. ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/doj-extends-digital-accessibility-compliance-dates-under-title-ii-of-the-ada/), [titleii.org](https://titleii.org/title-ii-updates))
- **Risk runs both ways.** An NFB win restores April 2026 (already passed) for large entities and 2027 for small ones. DOJ rulemaking could weaken the standard and cut urgency.

## 4. State laws

- **Colorado HB21-1110**
  - Enforcement began July 1, 2025, when the HB24-1454 good-faith period ended.
  - There is a private right of action, with injunctive relief and actual damages or **$3,500 per violation**. Under SB23-244, a violation on a single digital product (such as a website or app) counts as one incident.
  - As of Jan to Mar 2026, vendor and advocate sources reported **no publicly documented HB21-1110 lawsuits**. ([Converge](https://convergeaccessibility.com/2026/03/17/red-alert/), [CurbCut](https://www.curbcutaccessibility.com/ada-website-lawsuit/colorado/))
  - Claimed settlements for Denver Public Schools ($58k) and Jefferson County ($45k) are **[unverified; likely unreliable]**.
  - Observed Colorado behavior is document removal (Clear Creek) and full-PDF remediation contracts (Platte River).
- **Illinois, California and Minnesota.** I found no evidence on these in this scan **[not researched; gap]**. One vendor post mentions a California special-district "hardship" resolution process. ([link](https://eseospace.com/blog/special-district-website-budget-california/))
- **Section 504 (HHS).** HHS's separate web deadline is May 11, 2027. This matters for entities receiving HHS funds, such as health departments and colleges. ([Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/doj-extends-public-entities-compliance-deadline-ada-related-website-accessibility-hhss-may-2026-deadline-still-looms))

## 5. Budgets and who's winning

- **CivicPlus dominates the evidence.** It is the incumbent CMS vendor, selling Doc Access plus AudioEye as add-ons to existing contracts. Examples: Tuscaloosa, Clark County, Jurupa Valley, Rogers County, Whittier, Piedmont, Quincy, Bellingham, Hoover AL and St. Clair County AL.
- **Meeting video.** Granicus wins on meeting video (Elko, Hesperia).
- **Specialists also win:** Optimal (Washington state), Revise (Umatilla), Accessiblü (Story), National Captioning Institute (Douglas County).
- **Not seen.** I found no 2026 local award naming Level Access or Allyant.
- **Vendor-published cost ranges** (rough):
  - $15k to $75k for initial compliance of a local government site
  - $1,250 to $2,750 for an audit ([accessibility.works](https://www.accessibility.works/blog/ada-title-ii-website-compliance-cities-towns/))
- **Affordability strain:** see the Verdict (Story, Nye, Umatilla, Jurupa Valley); Rogers County's approval also hinged on a procurement check.

## Implications for a solo done-for-you newcomer

1. **The buying unit is small.** Most deals run $7.5k to $60k a year. Price fixed packages (for example, a document batch, the agenda-packet pipeline, a caption-correction retainer) to stay under local small-purchase thresholds, so purchases can skip a formal RFP. Thresholds vary by state; check each one.
2. **Compete against the incumbent tools, not other agencies.**
   - CivicPlus and AudioEye get bought because they are already on contract.
   - Pitch "human-verified, WCAG 2.1 AA-tested output with a conformance report" as a complement to automated tools.
   - The gaps those tools leave are tables, forms, scanned agenda packets, and caption QA. Elko County staff now edit AI-generated captions themselves, which is work you could take over.
3. **Target by deadline.**
   - Entities of 50k+ (cities, counties, school districts, colleges) need delivery before Apr 26, 2027, so close deals Nov 2026 to Q1 2027.
   - Health-funded entities face the HHS May 2027 date.
   - Colorado entities face current liability under HB21-1110.
   - Small towns and special districts (2028) are a 2027 pipeline. Many will default to removing content.
4. **Sell triage first.** Removal is the cheap alternative (Clear Creek, Bismarck); a low-cost "archive, remove, or fix" audit turns it into paid remediation of high-traffic documents.
5. **Hedge the legal risk.** An NFB win raises urgency. DOJ substantive rulemaking could cut it. Keep contracts short and per-deliverable.
