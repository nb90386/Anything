# 14 — YC, Thiel Fellowship and Investor Assessment

This is a fundability assessment, not a probability of acceptance. Program facts come from `data/yc-thiel-context.md`, which was reconstructed from secondary sources because ycombinator.com and thielfellowship.org could not be fetched; verify on the primary pages before acting.

## Program facts that matter now

- **YC Winter 2027** applications closed November 2, 2026 at 8pm PT, the day before this report. YC now runs four batches a year; later-batch dates are not announced. Late applications are reportedly considered without a guaranteed response date. Standard deal reported as $500K ($125K for 7% on a post-money SAFE plus $375K uncapped MFN SAFE). No formal age limit found; nothing found on minors signing SAFEs (UNVERIFIED). Solo founders are accepted at a higher bar.
- **YC W27 Requests for Startups:** "AI for City Hall" and "Plaid for Government Benefits" (government workflows), "AI for the Air-Gapped Economy" and "Own Your Intelligence" (private, owned intelligence). The thesis fits "AI for City Hall" loosely. Fit with an RFS is not a selection criterion and was not used in scoring.
- **Thiel Fellowship:** age 22 or younger at application; $250,000 over two years, no equity (from the 2026 class); fellows must leave or pause formal education for the two years; 12 fellows in 2026.
- **Alternatives:** Z Fellows (one week; optional $10K at a $1B cap), 1517 Fund Medici Grant ($1K+ no strings). Emergent Ventures and a16z speedrun terms not verified.

## YC view

| YC question | Honest answer today | What would change it by day 90 |
|---|---|---|
| What problem? | Public bodies must publish documents blind residents can use; auto-tagging output is unverified and the buyer stays liable. | — |
| Who needs it desperately? | Unproven. Evidence of pain exists (counties posting fewer documents; 2026 RFPs; 37% conformance), but no buyer has said they would pay for verification. | ≥2 paid pilots or ≥1 paying vendor (12, T2/T4). |
| What have you built? | A working prototype verifier and a reproducible benchmark (09): semantic checks caught defects that syntax checks missed. | A live product used weekly by 3–5 organizations. |
| Evidence of demand? | None yet. | Paid pilots, repeat use, inbound from the public benchmark. |
| Why this founder? | Fast builder with full-stack and AI-workflow experience; proximity to blind-user communities and to his own Title II college; willing to do the unscalable labeling work. Domain expertise in accessibility: none yet. | Co-authored benchmark with blind testers; credibility in the accessibility community. |
| Why now? | Title II dates (2027/2028); free auto-tagging floods the market with unverified output; VLM judging now costs cents per page. | — |
| How does it become very large? | Only by expanding beyond US public documents into private-sector mandates (EAA, Title III, HHS 504) and web/app verification. Possible, not probable (06). | Evidence that vendors and private buyers pay for the same verifier. |
| Biggest unresolved risk? | That verification is a feature, not a product, and an incumbent ships it first. | T2/T4 results. |

YC verdict: **not fundable today.** With T1 passing and a few paying customers, it becomes a credible application for a later 2027 batch. A solo 17-year-old needs strong evidence of execution to clear the higher solo bar.

## Thiel Fellowship view

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Unconventional vision? | Moderately. "Every public document should be machine-verified to work for blind people, and machine-made accessibility needs a neutral auditor" is clear and important but not radically contrarian. |
| Independent progress? | The prototype, the benchmark, and (if done) the blind-user study are real progress for a 17-year-old. |
| Difficult problem? | Hard in measurement and trust, not in deep technology. |
| Original insight? | The verification gap, measured. Partly anticipated by the PDF Association's August 2026 article. |
| Next two years? | A labeled corpus, a recognized benchmark, and a verification product used by vendors and public bodies. |

Thiel requires leaving school. **Do not leave college based on this concept.** Leaving is only worth considering if the 90-day gates pass and the company has paying customers and a clear reason why full-time work changes the outcome. The founder is 17, so the age limit leaves five years.

## Skeptical institutional investor view

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why should this company exist? | Because the party that generates accessibility fixes cannot credibly verify them, and the liable buyer has no tool to check. |
| Why wouldn't an incumbent win? | It might. Siteimprove (owns inventory), Adobe (owns the editor), PAC/axes4 (owns the de facto checker) and Allyant/AbleDocs (sell guarantees) can all add semantic checks. The startup's only answers are neutrality, a blind-user-labeled corpus, and speed. |
| Where does durable value accumulate? | In the labeled corpus and benchmark position, if they are built and recognized, and in evidence histories inside customers' workflows. |
| Cost to serve? | Verification: $0.004–$0.06 per page in model cost (measured), high gross margin. Remediation: depends on human review; unprofitable above ~20–25% flagged pages at current prices (06). |
| What evidence changes the decision? | T1 agreement ≥85%; paying customers on either side; repeat monthly use; inbound from the benchmark; real page volumes at or above the base case. |
| What must happen before major funding? | $10K+ MRR with retention, a recognized benchmark, and a clear expansion signal into private-sector documents or web. |

Fundability today: **pre-product, pre-traction, not fundable by institutional investors.** Fellowship and grant programs (1517 Medici, Z Fellows, Emergent Ventures if available) are appropriate for funding the blind-user testing and early work without leaving school.
