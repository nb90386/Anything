# Diligence: Demand and Page Volume for CA Public-Entity PDF Remediation

Retrieved 2026-11-03. Search-only research; pages outside GitHub could not be opened, so facts come from search snippets. **[U]** = unverified or my estimate.

## 0. Deadline check (this affects the whole thesis)

- DOJ's interim final rule (Federal Register, 2026-04-20) pushed the Title II web deadlines back one year. Entities of **50k or more must comply by April 26, 2027**. Entities **under 50k, and all special district governments, have until April 26, 2028**. WCAG 2.1 AA is still the standard. [FR]
- NFB has sued DOJ/HHS to set the extension aside (May 2026), so the date could move in either direction. [GovExec]
- **School and community college districts may not be in the 2027 tier.** EdSource reports that "smaller institutions such as community colleges have until 2028." One vendor says "nearly every school district" falls in the later tier. How Census population is assigned to CA K-12 and community college districts is **[U]**. Before treating education as a 2027 market, check 28 CFR 35.104 ("total population") with counsel.
- **Exceptions shrink the backlog.** Documents posted before the compliance date are exempt unless currently used to access a service; qualifying archives are exempt. [ockem; Berkeley DAP] The market is (a) documents in active use (forms, plans, policies) and (b) the **flow of new documents after the deadline**, mostly agenda packets, not the whole archive.

### Estimated annual new-document flow **[U: my estimate, not sourced]**

A model, not observed counts. Before pricing, the founder should open 10 real packets and count pages (about an hour).

| Entity type | Bodies / meetings per year | Pages per packet | New agenda pages per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large city (300k+; SJ, Oakland, SF) | Council ~35–45 plus committees ~100; Planning ~24; 20–40 commissions ~10 each | Council 300–1,000; committee 50–200; commission 30–150 | **40,000–120,000** |
| Mid city (75–200k) | Council ~22–24; Planning ~20; 8–15 commissions ~10 each | Council 150–500; PC 50–300; commission 20–80 | **10,000–30,000** |
| Small city (50–75k) | Council ~22; PC ~20; 5–10 commissions | Council 100–300 | **5,000–12,000** |
| County Board of Supervisors | ~35–45 Board meetings plus committees, Planning, and many commissions | 300–1,500+ | **30,000–100,000+** |
| K-12 / CCD board | ~20–24 regular meetings plus special meetings | 100–400 (BoardDocs/AgendaOnline attachments) | **3,000–10,000** |

At $3–5/page, a mid-size city's agenda flow is about $30k–150k per year; Jurupa Valley suggests many cities will pick tools or in-house work instead. **Brown Act timing:** agendas post 72 hours before meetings, so packet remediation needs 24–48-hour turnaround at volume, which is hard for one person.

### Public statements on hosted PDF counts
No Bay Area entity publicly stated a count like "we have 20,000 PDFs"; the nearest are Contra Costa (400k pages) and Exeter (30k pages). Berkeley and Union City say "some PDFs and other documents are not accessible for screen readers."

## 3. Evidence of unmet demand and frustration in California

- **Procurement is happening:** Contra Costa 2025 RFP (400k pages), Exeter 2026 RFP, Jurupa Valley 2026 CivicPlus contract, LACCD RFP 22-05 (2022). Formal RFPs favor vendors with insurance, references, and capacity, which disadvantages a solo founder.
- **Capacity gaps admitted:** Exeter (no inventory), Jurupa Valley (manual remediation infeasible), Berkeley and Union City (inaccessible PDFs).
- **Community colleges:** CCCO memo ESS 26-17 (Feb 27, 2026) sets WCAG 2.1 AA as the minimum, cites **Section 508 and Gov. Code 7405/11135** as applying to CCCs, and requires accessibility in procurement. ESS 26-38 (May 5, 2026) says to "continue advancing" despite the extension. The system-funded CCC Accessibility Center and the ACMM model may compete with paid remediation **[U]**.
- **Risk pools:** California JPIA (newsletter #170, April 2026) told members that deadlines moved but obligations are unchanged, with posted PDFs, Word files, and slides in scope.
- **League of California Cities / CSAC:** I found no guidance or cost surveys from either (gap).
- **Lawsuits:** No 2024–26 suit against a CA public entity over PDFs found. CA website suits moved to state court under Unruh ($4,000 per violation) but target businesses.

## 4. California law exposure beyond Title II

- **Gov. Code 11135**: bars disability discrimination in state-funded programs and incorporates ADA standards, so it reaches most local entities; 11139 gives a civil action. *(From my knowledge, [U]; CCCO confirms it applies to CCCs.)*
- **Gov. Code 7405**: requires Section 508 compliance by state governmental entities. CCCO says it applies to community colleges. Application to cities and counties is **[U]**.
- **AB 434 / Gov. Code 11546.7**: **state agencies only**; biennial director/CIO certification of WCAG 2.0 AA or later. Vendors claim WCAG 2.2 is now targeted **[U]**.
- **AB 2190 (2025–26)**: Unruh cure-period defense plus vendor liability; inactive file 2026-05-21, dead this session; aimed at businesses.
- **Unruh against public entities**: covers "business establishments"; reach to government **[U]**. The realistic hooks are 11135/11139 and Civil Rights Department complaints.

## Bottom line for the thesis
Volume is real (Contra Costa about 400k PDF pages; a 10k city about 30k). Buyer behavior is the problem: exceptions shrink the backlog, cities like Jurupa Valley buy about $12k/year tools instead of $3–5/page labor, counties buy through RFPs, and education may be 2028-tier. The best opening is likely the **recurring agenda-packet flow** of mid-size cities (about 10–30k pages per year) as a fast-turnaround service. Validate with real packet counts and clerk interviews.
