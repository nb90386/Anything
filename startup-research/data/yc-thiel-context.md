## A. YC Requests for Startups

### A1. Winter 2027 RFS (published 27 Oct 2026; 11 requests; "signed by 6 partners")

Framing line, quoted by multiple outlets: **"AI's biggest bottlenecks are moving beyond the models."** YC says it wants "the hardware, infra, financial systems, and specialized software to bring AI into the real economy, from factories and data centers to government and space." Coverage notes consumer and crypto categories are absent ("zero consumer, zero crypto"), and only one request (Plaid for Government Benefits) mentions AI agents. [Sources: linas.substack, fourweekmba, the-founders-corner, kucoin, postdigitalist]

The 11 titles (order as listed in a 27 Oct 2026 read of ycombinator.com/rfs reproduced in a GitHub file [lanaccelerator/lan-skills]):

1. **Startups in a World of Abundant Space Access** — Ankit Gupta. Cites SpaceX's projection of 10,000 Starship launches a year and says expendable Starship is already close to reusable Falcon 9 on cost. YC asks what business models work "when launch costs fall 100x or more," and wants those companies started before costs actually drop. One critic calls this among the weakest-evidenced requests. [the-founders-corner, conferras, postdigitalist]
2. **The $15-an-Hour Expert** — Brad Flora. AI lets capable people without credentials do specialist work: benefits, tax, insurance claims, medical billing, immigration paperwork. "The company is the workforce, not the software." Framing: "The last industrial revolution built a middle class from factory jobs; this one could build it from expert jobs." [the-founders-corner, conferras]
3. **Robots Made in America** — Diana Hu. The US installed 38,400 industrial robots in 2025 (+12%), mostly imported; China made 94% of sintered rare-earth magnets in 2024. The FCC now authorizes new connected mobile robot models only if US-made or exempt, with >65% domestic component cost rising to 75% by 2029; "Assembly alone doesn't qualify." Focus is the parts: magnets, motors, gears, sensors, battery packs. [the-founders-corner, x.com/stretchcloud]
4. **LLMs That Write Well** — Jared Friedman. Models got better at code and math and worse at writing; reward signals that boost coding/agentic performance "wash out distinct voice and style," so newer models sound more robotic. YC wants models specialized in writing people enjoy. [the-founders-corner; YC YouTube Shorts description via ZacSadan/podcasts-summary]
5. **Plaid for Government Benefits** — Gustaf Alströmer (anchor: ycombinator.com/rfs#plaid-for-government-benefits). A consent-based data layer that pulls verified income, tax and residency records from the source and reuses them across programs: "Verify once, apply everywhere." Cites GAO's estimate that fraud may cost the federal government up to half a trillion dollars a year, and that from 2027 states must re-check millions of Medicaid enrollees every six months. Envisions agents that manage benefits enrollment. [beinsure, colrows, vclens, Yashsharma1911/granted]
6. **Wall Street for the Physical Buildout** — Brad Flora. Financing instruments that don't exist yet: lenders for robots that each earn ~$500k/yr, put options on GPU capacity, leasing companies and hedges. Every past buildout (rail, electricity, telecom) created large financial companies. [the-founders-corner, conferras]
7. **Vertical Foundation Models** — attributed to Diana Hu **[unverified; one brief says "Diana Hu cites Jev's typed decisions"]**. Specialized models for specific industries; a Chinese-language summary says the hard part is obtaining high-quality, mathematically verifiable, industry-specific reward signals. [CharlieChenyuZhang/news-watchdog, chaincatcher]
8. **AI for City Hall** — Gustaf Alströmer. Plan review, zoning checks, inspections, licensing and public records still happen largely by hand; "In some cities, getting a building permit takes longer than building the house." ~19,000 US cities; if permits took 10% of current time, cities would gain ~$16B/yr in tax revenue. Called "one of the biggest untouched markets for AI." [the-founders-corner, eric0205market-bit/market-intelligence, YC Shorts description]
9. **Data Center Supply Chain** — Jared Friedman. The AI data-center buildout is the largest US capital project in history; large power transformers (~3-year lead time), switchgear, breakers and liquid cooling are sold out; grain-oriented electrical steel for transformer cores comes from a single US plant. [the-founders-corner, x.com/stretchcloud]
10. **AI for the Air-Gapped Economy** — author **[not found]**. Bring frontier AI to settings where data can't leave the building: defense contractors, banks, hospitals, nuclear plants. Build on-prem models, deployment tooling, security accreditation and hardware. [YC Shorts description; lanaccelerator/lan-skills]
11. **Own Your Intelligence** — author **[not found]**. Coverage groups it with the "owned and vertical foundation models" theme: models a company can own, specialize and run privately rather than rent. [vclens, humanaios-ios/operations]

Partner count check: five named authors (Gupta, Flora, Hu, Friedman, Alströmer); coverage says six partners signed, so one partner (likely on items 10 or 11) is unidentified.

Themes (as coverage frames them): verified information and consent-based data (5); intelligence inside real-world systems and government workflows (5, 8); specialized/vertical models and models that write (4, 7); private, locally owned intelligence (10, 11); physical tech and reindustrialization — robots, data-center hardware, space, and the financing to fund it (1, 3, 6, 9). One commentator: "seven of the 11 are about hardware, financing, government and work, and the four about models ask for models a company can own or specialise."

Application deadline tied to this RFS: 2 Nov 2026, 8pm PT.

### A2. Summer 2026 RFS (15 requests)
Per Modelence (which cites ycombinator.com/rfs): AI-Native Service Companies; Company Brain; Dynamic Software Interfaces; Hardware Supply Chain; SaaS Challengers; Software for Agents; Startups That Want to Sell to Huge Companies; Supply Chain 2.0 for Semiconductors; The AI Operating System for Companies; AI for Low-Pesticide Agriculture; AI Personalized Medicine; Counter-Swarm Defense; Electronics in Space; Industrial Capabilities in Space; Inference Chips for Agent Workflows. Dominant themes: "Company Brain" (internal knowledge as AI-executable maps), AI-native service companies that sell outcomes not software, inference chips for agents. **[conflicting]** One tracker counts 16. A Fall 2026 edition (13 ideas, incl. "AI for the Aging Population" by Max Kolysh) also appeared between Summer and Winter. [modelence, thevccorner, superframeworks, linas note, admin-gltrax doc]

### A3. Spring 2026 RFS (7 ideas; some trackers say 10)
Consistently named: Cursor for Product Managers; AI-Native Hedge Funds; AI-Native Agencies; Stablecoin Financial Services; AI for Government; Modern Metal Mills; AI Guidance for Physical Work. Some lists add Infra for Government Fraud Hunters, large spatial reasoning models, and tools that make LLM training easier. **[conflicting count]** [modelence, the-ai-corner, LinkedIn joseph-michael, superframeworks]

Trajectory: Spring 2026 → agent-era software and vertical "AI-native" firms; Summer 2026 → agent infrastructure, hardware/semis, space and defense; Winter 2027 → almost entirely "beyond the model": physical supply chains, financing, government, owned/specialized models.

## B. YC application facts (W27 and later)

- **W27 deadline:** 2 Nov 2026, 8:00pm PT (YC's own X post: "Apply by November 2"). One aggregator says 3 Nov **[conflicting; likely time-zone]**. Late applications are considered but with no guaranteed response date. [x.com/ycombinator, roundfunded, zyner]
- **Decisions:** on-time applicants hear by 11 Dec 2026; interviews by video in Nov–Dec 2026. [roundfunded]
- **Batch:** W27 runs January–March 2027, in person in San Francisco. S26 ran July–September in SF. [venturecapitaltracker, urbangeekz]
- **Cadence:** four batches a year (Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall). Spring/Summer/Fall 2027 dates are not announced; third-party calendars are extrapolating. Some older guides still describe 2 or 3 batches **[outdated]**. [roundfunded, zyner]
- **Standard deal:** $500,000 total = $125,000 for 7% on a post-money SAFE + $375,000 on an uncapped SAFE with an MFN clause (converts at the best terms of the next priced round). Structure dates to Jan 2022 and is described as unchanged in 2026; terms live at ycombinator.com/deal. Older pages quoting $150K are stale. [zyner, roundfunded, Wikipedia, founderstoday]
- **Early Decision:** one source says YC "offers an Early Decision track that allows founders to apply ahead of time and lock in a spot for a future batch." **[single source; not confirmed]** [joinleland]
- **What YC says it looks for:** sources describe no formal requirements for industry, founder age or education; the application weighs founders, progress and clarity. (Only secondary summaries were found; no primary text.)
- **Solo founders:** accepted but face a higher bar; estimates of 10–15% of a batch; "90% of the companies that make it through YC have co-founders." Batch data: S26 solo founders rose from 5% to 19% (one source); W26 reports 17% (36 companies) vs. another count of 22; P26/X26 13% (26 companies). **[conflicting]** [ycinsight, extruct, foundedceo, roundfunded]
- **Very young founders / 17-year-olds:** no formal age limit found; one site says most applicants are 18+ but high-schoolers have been accepted "if they show seriousness"; claims about the youngest admit range from 14 (anecdote) to 18 to 22 **[conflicting, unverified]**. Nothing found on minors signing SAFEs or attending the program. [quora, admissionsight, ycinsight]
- **Batch composition:** S26 ~233–235 companies; AI companies ~60% of the cohort (up from ~40% two years earlier); hard tech 8% → 20%. W26: 199 companies in one dataset. Average founder age: not published in any result found. [neweconomies, foundra, roundfunded, extruct]
- **Acceptance rate:** no source gives an official figure; widely repeated "~1%" estimates were not surfaced with sourcing, so treat any number as unverified.

## C. Thiel Fellowship

- **Eligibility:** 22 or younger at the time of application (2025 class release: "All fellows selected ... were 22 or younger when applying"). An older Unigo page says 23 **[outdated]**. Fellows must "drop out or skip college for the duration of the two-year program" / be "willing to drop out of or pause formal education." Two years = the fellowship period during which fellows work full-time on their project (the 2026 class "gathered in San Francisco in late April 2026 to begin their two-year fellowship period"). [businesswire 2025, fundsforngos, causo, for9a, catalaize]
- **Grant:** $250,000 over two years, no equity, starting with the class of 2026 (up from $100,000; 2024 class received $100K). Some pages still show $100K or $200K **[outdated]**. [Wikipedia, pulse2, richnerds, fundsforngos]
- **Process/timing:** sources conflict — "rolling basis without fixed deadlines" vs. listed deadlines of 31 Jul 2026 and 31 Dec 2026 **[conflicting]**. Classes are announced in spring (20 Mar 2024; 22 May 2025; 21 Apr 2026). [fellows.best, for9a, businesswire]
- **What reviewers look for:** no primary text found. Coverage describes the 2026 class as "smaller and more focused on hard tech" (robotics, AI, fintech, BCI) and says fellows commit to full-time work and get a network of founders, investors and scientists. [catalaize, richnerds]
- **2026 class (12 fellows, announced 21 Apr 2026):** Aubrey Niederhoffer (NY) — Swoop, Africa super app starting with food delivery in Nigeria; Claire Wang (LA) — simulations of nervous systems starting with C. elegans; Alex Shieh (Salem, NH) — The Antifraud Company; Harry O'Connor (Cork) — Sentient Machines, robotics foundation-model lab. Fellows also from Brazil, Poland, India. [richnerds, catalaize, tryspecter]
- **2025 class (announced 22 May 2025; joined 290 prior fellows):** Ferdinand Dabitz (Berlin) — Ivy, global instant payments; Jackson Denka (LA) — Azura, on-chain trading platform; Carlo Kobe (Hamburg) — Fizz, AI financial advisor; Jennifer Lin (Miami) — accelerated access to cures for rare diseases; Koki Mashita (Tokyo) — Aeolus, planetary defense for extreme weather; Juan Ebrath (Bogotá) — Phase, whole-body regeneration; Elias Fizesan (Luxembourg) — Canopy Labs, digital-human foundation models; Soren Monroe-Anderson (Anchorage) — Neros, defense drones; Aidan Smith (Mountain View) — Innerphases, communication devices. Partial list. [x.com/thielfellowship, businesswire 2025]
- **Criticism / trade-offs:** not researched (search budget exhausted). Known structural trade-off from the terms themselves: the grant requires leaving or pausing school for two years; the class is very small (12 in 2026), so odds are low.

### Alternatives for very young founders (verified terms only)
- **Z Fellows:** one-week, mostly virtual program with a final in-person day (SF or NY); optional $10,000 investment converting at the next priced round with a $1B valuation cap; can participate without the money. One aggregator lists "1% equity" **[conflicting]**. [theincubatorpro, startupgrantsindia, foundersprime]
- **1517 Fund Medici Grant:** $1,000+ no-strings grant (no equity, no IP claim), rolling email applications; favors high-school/college students or dropouts on non-credit projects, North America-based. 1517 separately invests $50K–$1M. [grantedai, grantcompass, 1517.substack]
- **Emergent Ventures (Mercatus):** exists as a grant program (several GitHub projects credit it); amounts not found. **[unverified]**
- **a16z speedrun:** described as a16z's early-stage cohort accelerator; terms not found. **[unverified]**
- **Neo, Contrary, Pear, Soma:** not researched; no verified terms.
