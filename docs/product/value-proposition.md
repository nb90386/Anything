# Value Proposition: Malbek Revenue Intelligence and Contract Risk Command Center

> Independent portfolio demo, not affiliated with Malbek Inc.

## The one-line value prop

**Malbek already helps teams manage contracts. This shows an intelligence layer that turns contract data into board-level business decisions: revenue at risk this quarter, contracts needing executive review, non-standard clauses by business unit, and approval bottlenecks slowing deal velocity, in one connected view instead of four separate reports.**

## The business problem

Contract data is real, but it is scattered across three disconnected places inside most CLM deployments: a repository (what was signed), a workflow tool (what's pending), and a set of reports someone builds when an executive asks a question. By the time a Finance or Legal team can answer "how much revenue are we losing to unenforced price escalators this quarter," the answer has already cost someone a week of manual contract review.

Industry research consistently puts the number at 8 to 9 percent of contract value lost to poor post-signature management (WorldCC, Deloitte, McKinsey estimates, all in that range). That is not a data problem. Every CLM platform on the market, including Malbek's own BusinessIQ, Icertis's Vera Analytics, and Sirion's Optimization Insights, already extracts this data and already claims to detect leakage. The gap is not detection. The gap is that the output lands as a dashboard, a heatmap, or a quarterly report that someone has to go looking for, not as a number an executive sees the moment they open the tool.

## What this demo actually does differently

It does not claim to out-detect BusinessIQ, Vera, or Optimization Insights. It claims something narrower and more honest: the same underlying signals (leakage, risk, clause drift, negotiation patterns) belong on one continuous surface, not four disconnected exports. Concretely:

- **Revenue at risk this quarter** is the first number on the screen, not a report you generate.
- **Contracts needing executive review** are a ranked, clickable list, not a filtered table you have to sort yourself.
- **Non-standard clauses by business unit** show up as a visual pattern (which teams are drifting from the playbook, and how fast), not a static compliance checklist.
- **Approval bottlenecks slowing deal velocity** are visible as a place contracts get stuck, tied to the actual dollar value sitting behind that bottleneck.

Every one of those numbers is clickable down to the exact contract and clause that produced it. That traceability, carried over from the v0.1 build's core discipline, is the actual differentiator: nothing is asserted that can't be proven on the spot.

| Role | The question they actually ask | What this demo shows them first |

| **Legal** | "Which contracts have language that doesn't match our playbook, and is it getting worse over time?" | Clause Drift Analyzer: playbook deviation plus a trend line across amendments, not just a point-in-time flag |

## Why now

Two things happened at once in 2025-2026 that make this the right moment for this specific demo. First, revenue leakage and clause deviation detection became table-stakes claims across the CLM market (Malbek, Icertis, Sirion all launched named products for this within the same 12-month window). Second, the market's own commentary is starting to say the quiet part out loud: the underlying AI capability across mature vendors is converging, and the real gap is operational, how the intelligence is delivered and acted on, not whether it can be extracted. That is exactly the gap this demo is built to close: not new detection technology, but a genuinely different delivery surface for signals the market already agrees matter.

## Why this is additive to a CLM, not competing with one

A CLM's job is to move a contract from request to signature to renewal, reliably, with the right approvals and the right paper trail. That is infrastructure. This demo does not attempt to replace that infrastructure, it assumes it exists and asks a different question: once the infrastructure has produced clean, structured contract data, what does the best possible *decision layer* on top of that data look like? The Risk Radar, Revenue Leakage Detector, Clause Drift Analyzer, and Negotiation Intelligence briefs are all read-only intelligence over data a CLM like Malbek's already collects. Nothing here proposes a new approval engine, a new e-signature flow, or a new contract repository. The pitch to a CLM company's own leadership is specifically that this is what their product looks like one layer up: the same contracts, reasoned about as a portfolio instead of one document at a time.
